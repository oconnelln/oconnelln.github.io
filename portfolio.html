Teaching Portfolio

I take a student-centered approach to my teaching by creating an inclusive and engaging space for students to learn and make connections with the world outside the classroom. Students explore identity and power within communication, gain digital technology skills, and learn how to position themselves for future opportunities.

For example, I devote time in almost every course I teach to digital accessibility. Students recognize that everyone in class has different information needs and preferences, explore best practices for digital accessibility, incorporate that into their projects, and reflect on how they can attend to accessibility outside of class. These courses allow students to understand the effects of writing in the world and that their writing holds power — which makes these courses a joy to teach.

My passion for course design has allowed me to incorporate themes of community responsibility, technology, and students' futures in courses ranging from basic writing, first-year composition, professional and technical writing including web design, first-year seminars on multimodality and information design, and courses on professional development and career preparation. The full list of courses I have taught is on this webpage!

Ultimately, I encourage students to view themselves as responsible communicators and engaged civic participants as well as to consider how their classwork connects to possible futures.

Writing, Communication, & Design

Introduction to Web Design (ENGLISH 391c, UMass Amherst)

Introduction to web design where students learn HTML5 and CSS to create web pages from scratch, gain familiarity with practices that go into planning a website, and learn best practices for accessible web design.

Introduction to Professional Writing (ENGLISH 379, UMass Amherst)

Overview of the field of professional writing, with a critical AI focus, where students practice genres in professional writing, develop research skills and gain experience in communicating specialized information to non-specialists, and reflect on the social role of the professional writer.

Technical Communication (ENL 266, UMass Dartmouth)

Introduction to technical communication skills used in business and industry. Students practice techniques for creating, managing, and presenting information in written, oral, visual, and electronic forms.

Technical Communication google doc syllabus

Designing for Humans (FYS 191HFA, new course, UMass Amherst)

First-year seminar where students collaboratively problem-solve with community partners and consider concepts of UX, usability, accessibility, and user- and human-centered design within information design.

Making Meaning Through Design (FYS 191HFA, new course, UMass Amherst)

First-year seminar where students learn about best practices for information design based on messages we want to convey, modes and mediums we want to use to deliver our messages, and our audiences we want to reach.

Creating Video Games for Social Impact (FYS 191HFA, new course, UMass Amherst)

First-year seminar where students discuss video games in society, play games, explore social justice aspects, and move step-by-step through the process of creating their own games that promote positive change.
